### The Vision: From Solitary to Social Learning
Intelligent Tutoring Systems (ITS) have traditionally focused on a dyadic interaction: one machine teaching one student. While effective, this ignores a fundamental pillar of pedagogy: **Collaborative Learning**.

My PhD thesis, *Conversational Agents Integration in Intelligent Tutoring Systems*, aims to break this isolation. I am architecting a new generation of ITS where **AI Agents act as Peers**, not just tutors.

> **Core Hypothesis:** By simulating indistinguishable, distinct virtual students, we can trigger the "Learning by Teaching" and "Peer Modeling" effects, significantly improving student engagement and outcomes compared to traditional tutoring.

### Research Lines & Objectives

My research is structured around three key pillars, moving from reactive systems to proactive, social intelligence:

#### 1. The "Virtual Student" Architecture
Building on my TFM findings (*Be My Mate*), I am developing a generalized framework for **Virtual Peers**. These agents are not generic chatbots; they possess:
* **Distinct Personas:** Varying levels of competence (e.g., a "novice" agent that makes mistakes for the human to correct).
* **Long-term Memory:** Remembering past interactions to build rapport.
* **Agentic Behavior:** Using frameworks like **SPADE** and **LangChain** to deliberate before speaking.

#### 2. Proactive Assistance Forecasting
Traditional ITS are reactive (they wait for the student to fail). I am researching **Generative Approaches** to predict *when* a student needs help before they even ask.
* **Technique:** Using Markov Models and Transformer-based architectures to forecast user actions and intervene proactively.
* **Goal:** To smooth the friction in collaborative problem solving.

#### 3. Ethics & Multimodality in AI
As we introduce "fake" peers, ethical boundaries become critical. My work explores:
* **Transparency:** How to disclose AI identity without breaking immersion.
* **Bias Mitigation:** Ensuring virtual agents do not reinforce gender or cultural stereotypes in educational settings.
